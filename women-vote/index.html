Bangladesh 2026 · How men and women voted

Women voted more for Jamaat

In Bangladesh, men and women often vote at separate centres. So we can count their votes separately. We looked at 11,096 such centres. One party did better with women than with men — and it was not the BNP.

1 · Where the numbers come from

One in four centres tells us how women voted

Most polling centres are mixed, so we cannot tell a man's vote from a woman's. But 11,096 centres are single-sex — about a quarter of all centres, and a quarter of all voters. Those are the ones we use.

2 · The main finding

The BNP got the same from both. Jamaat did not.

The BNP's share is almost exactly the same among men and women. Jamaat's is 1.7 points higher among women — the only big party to gain.

3 · Is it just religious parties?

No — the other Islamic party did worse with women

If women simply preferred religious parties, Islami Andolan would have gained too. It did not — it lost ground. So did the others. Jamaat is the only party that women supported more than men did.

4 · Could it just be location?

We checked men and women in the same building

Maybe women's centres simply sit in places that like Jamaat. To rule that out, we took only buildings that had both a men's centre and a women's centre — 2,453 pairs. Same village, same street, same voters. The only difference is who voted.

The gap stayed: +1.0 point on average inside those pairs. Do the same test on the BNP and the difference is zero. That is the important part — the method does not invent gaps.

5 · But not everywhere

The gap is small in most places, and big in the south-west

This is not a country-wide shift. In a typical seat, men and women voted almost the same. The gap comes mostly from one region — the Jessore–Satkhira–Jhenaidah belt, where women push Jamaat from 46% to past 51%.

6 · The same region won Jamaat its seats

The south-west gave Jamaat most of its wins

That belt is small — only 10% of all seats. But Jamaat won 20 of the 30 seats there, which is 27% of all its victories. Everywhere else it won just one seat in five. So its strongest region and its biggest gender gap are the same place.

7 · The key test

It is not only about that one region

A fair question: is this just "Jamaat is strong in Khulna", counted twice? So we split the seats both ways at once. Even outside the belt, the women's gap is four times bigger in seats Jamaat won than in seats it lost — +2.1 points against +0.5.

In other words, winning and the women's gap travel together across the whole country — not just in the heartland.

8 · One more thing

Fewer women voted — and they still chose Jamaat more

Women's turnout was about 5 points lower than men's. So this is not because more women came out. Fewer came, and they still gave Jamaat a bigger share. It is about choice, not turnout.

9 · What if only one side voted?

On women's votes alone, Jamaat leads more seats

Count each seat using only the men's centres, then only the women's. Jamaat leads 65 seats on men's votes and 77 on women's. In 16 seats, men choose the BNP but women choose Jamaat — only 4 go the other way.

In short

What to remember

  • Jamaat is the only big party women backed more than men — by 1.7 points. The BNP was the same for both.
  • It is not simply about religious parties. The other Islamic party, Islami Andolan, did worse with women.
  • It is not about location either. Comparing men and women in the same building gives the same answer; doing it for the BNP gives zero.
  • It is concentrated, not national. Almost no gap in a typical seat; a big one in the south-west, where women push Jamaat past 50%.
  • That region also gave Jamaat most of its seats — 20 of 30 there, 27% of all its wins from 10% of the seats.
  • And the link holds outside that region too. The women's gap is four times bigger in seats Jamaat won than lost, even away from the belt.
  • Fewer women voted, but they still chose Jamaat more — so it is choice, not turnout.
  • On women's votes alone, Jamaat leads 77 seats instead of 65 — 16 seats change hands.
